Research Interests

My mathematical interests lie primarily in algebraic and geometric topology, with a particular focus on knot theory and low-dimensional topology. I am especially interested in the topology of three- and four-dimensional manifolds, the algebraic invariants of knots and manifolds, and the interplay between topological and geometric structures. I am also interested in differential geometry and mathematical physics, particularly in topological quantum field theory, the connections between knot invariants and quantum field theory, and the geometry of Calabi–Yau manifolds in string theory. My research experience includes the study of the cohomology of homogeneous spaces and Stallings' fibration theorem for knot exteriors. These projects have shaped my interest in understanding how algebraic and geometric methods illuminate the structure of topological spaces. I aim to explore these connections further while remaining open to new research directions at the intersection of topology, geometry, and mathematical physics.

Cohomology of Homogeneous Spaces

In my Master's thesis at the University of Yaoundé I, supervised by Dr. Hilaire George Mbiakop , I studied the cohomology of homogeneous spaces, particularly Stiefel manifolds and complex Grassmannians. These spaces arise naturally in geometry and topology and provide important examples for understanding the relationships between geometric structures and algebraic invariants.

In this work, I studied their cohomological properties using the Gysin exact sequence and characteristic classes, particularly Euler and Chern classes. These tools provide a systematic way to relate the cohomology of a space to that of its base and fiber in suitable geometric settings, while characteristic classes encode important information about vector bundles.

Low-Dimensional Topology and Knot Theory

My MSc research at the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences (AIMS) Rwanda focused on Stallings' fibration theorem for knot exteriors , supervised by Professor Ana García Lecuona . This theorem provides a fundamental criterion for determining when the exterior of a knot in the three-sphere fibers over the circle, linking the topology of knot complements to the algebraic properties of their fundamental groups. A central aspect of my work was the study of the relationship between the fundamental group of a knot exterior, its commutator subgroup, and the topology of infinite cyclic covers. I also explored the role of Seifert surfaces and the algebraic techniques used to study these structures. This project deepened my interest in knot theory and low-dimensional topology, particularly in the interplay between algebraic invariants, covering spaces, and the geometric structure of three-dimensional manifolds.
